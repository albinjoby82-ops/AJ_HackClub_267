Dublin Micromouse Open 2026

Saturday 26 September 2026 UCD Village, University College Dublin Hosted by UCD ElecSoc

Ireland's first national Micromouse competition. Build it in six hours. Race it the same day.

The challenge

You're supposed to figure it out

Micromouse is one of the oldest challenges in robotics: build a small robot that solves a maze entirely on its own. No remote control, no driver, no human input once the run starts. The mouse has to map the maze, work out the fastest route to the centre, and drive it.

Most Micromouse competitions are won by machines that took months to build. Ours is not one of those.

At the Dublin Micromouse Open, every team gets six hours and the same box of parts. You design it, build it, program it and race it in a single day. The build sprint is the competition. Nobody arrives with a finished robot, which means a first year who has never touched a soldering iron lines up against a final year team on genuinely equal terms.

Who it's for

No experience needed. Just show up.

Engineering and computer science undergraduates, and anyone else who wants to have a go. You do not need robotics experience, you do not need to know embedded C, and you do not need to bring anything. Complete beginners are exactly who we built this for.

The kit

Same box of parts. Every team.

Every team receives an identical modular kit.

  • ESP32 microcontroller
  • Encoded motors
  • Motor driver
  • IMU
  • Distance sensors
  • Battery management system
  • Buck converter
  • Battery pack

The kit is modular on purpose. There is no single correct mouse design, and we're expecting to see very different machines come out of the same box of parts.

We prepare you properly

You are not handed a bag of components and left to it

Before the day

A pre-recorded video series covering everything you need to know about driving sensors with the ESP32, plus written documentation and setup docs published in advance, so your team can hit the ground running instead of losing an hour to environment setup.

On the day

A 30 minute opening briefing with all the electronics diagrams prepared for you, walking through how to use the encoded motors, the motor driver, the IMU and the distance sensors, why there's a battery management system in the kit, why there's a buck converter, and how the battery packs actually work. We also cover GitHub basics for anyone who hasn't used it, and talk through the different maze-solving strategies so you can pick an approach rather than guess at one.

Throughout

ElecSoc committee members and postgraduate demonstrators are on the floor all day. If you're stuck, someone will be standing next to you within a minute.

Practice mazes

Tune it against real walls

Full size and mini practice mazes are open all day, so you can tune your algorithm against real walls instead of finding out what's wrong during your timed run.

One of the mini mazes is built deliberately so that the fastest route cuts the diagonals and takes a straight line through. If your mouse only knows how to follow the grid, it will finish. If it can reason about the path, it will win. That maze is there so you can develop your decision-making properly before it counts.

Judging

Fastest verified run wins

The main award goes to the fastest verified run to the centre of the maze, with further awards for design and reliability.
