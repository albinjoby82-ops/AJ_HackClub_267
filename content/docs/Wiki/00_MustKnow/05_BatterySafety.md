# Battery Safety

Batteries can supply very high current. Lithium-ion and lithium-polymer batteries can ignite if they are shorted, crushed, punctured, overcharged, overheated or internally damaged.

## General rules

- Use the correct battery type, voltage and connector.
- Prevent terminals from contacting tools, jewellery or loose components.
- Use a fuse or appropriate protection where a pack can deliver dangerous fault current.
- Do not mix old and new cells or different cell types.
- Do not solder directly to a cell unless it was designed for that process and an approved procedure is available.
- Remove batteries from unattended prototypes where practical.

## Charging lithium batteries

- Use a charger specifically designed for the cell chemistry and series-cell count.
- Confirm charge current and polarity before connection.
- Charge on a non-combustible surface in the designated location.
- Keep the battery where it can be observed according to local rules.
- Do not charge a swollen, hot, leaking, punctured, crushed or otherwise damaged cell.
- Do not improvise a lithium charger from a bench supply.

{: .warning-title}
> Stop if a lithium battery changes
>
> If a cell swells, hisses, leaks, smells unusual, becomes unexpectedly hot or produces smoke, do not handle it unnecessarily. Disconnect power only if this can be done safely, move people away and raise the alarm.

Do not put a failing cell in a pocket, ordinary bin or sealed container. Ask a technician or organiser what to do next.

## Storage and transport

- Protect terminals against short circuit.
- Store cells in the designated location, away from heat and combustible material.
- Do not leave loose cells in drawers with metal parts.
- Inspect packs before and after use.
- Follow transport restrictions when taking batteries off site.

## Disposal

Never place batteries or electronic waste in general rubbish. Use the university's battery collection, a retailer take-back point or a civic amenity facility.

For disposal information, see the Irish EPA's [WEEE and battery guidance](https://www.epa.ie/our-services/compliance--enforcement/waste/weee/guidance-on-weee-and-batteries/).
