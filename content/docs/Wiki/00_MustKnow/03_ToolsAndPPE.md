# Tools, Making and PPE

Use the least hazardous tool that can do the job. A hand tool may be safer than a machine, but only if it is suitable, undamaged and used on a stable workpiece.

## Hand tools

- Use the correct size and type of screwdriver, cutter, drill bit or spanner.
- Clamp work instead of holding it towards your hand.
- Cut away from your body and other people.
- Wear eye protection when cutting wire, drilling, grinding or when fragments may fly.
- Do not use damaged handles, mushroomed striking tools or chipped blades.
- Retract or sheath blades immediately after use.
- Pass tools handle-first and never leave sharp tools hidden under material.

## Powered and rotating tools

- Complete the required induction before use.
- Inspect the tool, cable, guard and workholding before starting.
- Keep hair, sleeves, jewellery, gloves and cables away from rotating parts.
- Secure the work and remove adjustment keys before starting.
- Wait for all movement to stop before measuring, clearing waste or making adjustments.
- Disconnect or isolate power before changing blades, bits or accessories.

Gloves can protect against chemicals, heat or sharp material, but they can be caught by rotating machinery. Follow the tool-specific procedure.

## 3D printers

- Do not touch hot ends, beds or freshly heated parts.
- Keep clear of moving axes.
- Use approved materials and ventilation.
- Do not leave a printer running unless the local procedure allows unattended operation.
- Resin printing requires chemical controls described in [Chemicals, Adhesives and Resin](06_ChemicalSafety.md).

## Laser cutters and other restricted machines

Use only after local training. Use approved materials; some plastics can produce corrosive or toxic gases. Never defeat covers, extraction, interlocks or guards. Stay with the machine when required.

## Choosing PPE

PPE is the last layer, not a substitute for removing a hazard, guarding a tool or providing extraction.

Common club PPE includes:

- Safety glasses for flying fragments and splashes
- Task-appropriate gloves for chemicals or sharp material
- Hearing protection where the local assessment requires it
- Closed footwear in fabrication areas
- Protective clothing for messy or splash-prone work

Use the PPE specified by the machine instructions, room signage or chemical safety data sheet.
