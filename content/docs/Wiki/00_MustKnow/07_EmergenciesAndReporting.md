# Emergencies, First Aid and Reporting

Before a session begins, identify:

- The nearest exit and assembly point
- How to raise the alarm
- The local emergency contact
- The nearest first-aid kit, eyewash and AED
- The first-aid responder and event lead
- How electrical power can be isolated

## At UCD

Use the **UCD 24-hour Emergency Line: extension 7999 or 01 716 7999 from a mobile**. UCD Campus Services coordinates the emergency response and guides external services to the correct location.

For the Engineering Building, UCD lists the assembly area as **beside the lake, in front of the Confucius Institute**. Always follow current local signage and instructions because assembly arrangements can change.

See [UCD Emergency Response Procedures](https://www.ucd.ie/sirc/healthsafety/emergencyresponseprocedures/).

## Away from UCD

Use the venue's emergency procedure. In Ireland, call **112 or 999** for emergency services. Calls are free. Give the location, what happened, the number of people affected and a callback number. See [gov.ie guidance on contacting emergency services](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-the-taoiseach/services/how-to-contact-emergency-services-in-ireland/).

## If the alarm sounds or there is a fire

1. Stop work if this can be done immediately.
2. Raise the alarm.
3. Leave by the nearest safe exit.
4. Do not use lifts.
5. Go to the assembly point.
6. Do not re-enter until authorised.

Do not delay evacuation to collect equipment or fight a fire. Only a trained person should use firefighting equipment when the local procedure says it is safe.

## Injury or exposure

- Make the area safe without creating another casualty.
- Get a trained first aider.
- For electrical contact, isolate power before touching the casualty.
- For chemicals, follow the SDS and local eyewash/shower procedure.
- Seek emergency medical help for serious injury, breathing difficulty, loss of consciousness, significant electrical shock or rapidly worsening symptoms.

## Report incidents and near misses

Report injury, fire, spill, exposure, property damage and near misses to the event lead and through the venue's reporting system. A near miss is useful information about a control that almost failed.

UCD states that incidents resulting in injury, near misses or property damage must be notified to SIRC using its online system as soon as practicable. See [UCD SIRC](https://www.ucd.ie/sirc/).

Preserve relevant details without putting anyone at further risk:

- What happened and when
- Equipment and materials involved
- Immediate actions taken
- Witnesses
- Photos only when appropriate and permitted

Do not assign blame in the immediate response. Make the situation safe, support the people involved and improve the system.
