# Safety & Fundamentals

This section contains the minimum safety knowledge for Build Club projects. It is designed for practical, low-voltage electronics and small-scale making—not industrial research.

Safety is not about eliminating every interesting idea. It is about noticing what could hurt someone, choosing sensible controls and stopping when a project moves beyond the club's equipment or competence.

{: .warning-title}
> Local rules come first
>
> Follow the room's signs and instructions from technicians, staff and event organisers. This wiki supports local training; it does not replace it.

## The five Build Club rules

1. **Know how to make it safe.** Find the power switch, emergency exit and person in charge before starting.
2. **Power off before changing a circuit.** Inspect first, energise second.
3. **Use the lowest practical energy.** Prefer USB or current-limited low-voltage supplies.
4. **Use equipment only after instruction.** Guards, extraction and interlocks stay in place.
5. **Stop and report surprises.** Heat, smoke, swelling, unusual smells, damaged cables, injury and near misses are reasons to stop—not to try again faster.

## Projects that require specific approval

Do not begin these as ordinary drop-in club activities:

- Mains-voltage construction or exposed mains equipment
- Microwave, X-ray, high-power laser or ionising-radiation projects
- High-voltage capacitor banks, large batteries or high-current battery packs
- Pressure vessels, combustion, rockets, pyrotechnics or energetic materials
- Safety-critical lifting, vehicles or equipment intended to support a person
- Unfamiliar hazardous chemicals, biological agents or processes producing hazardous dust or fumes
- Defeating a machine guard, interlock or protective earth

Ask a technician or staff member before attempting one of these projects.
