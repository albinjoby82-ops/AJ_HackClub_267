# Chemicals, Adhesives and Resin

Build Club chemicals may include flux, isopropyl alcohol, lubricants, paints, spray adhesives, cyanoacrylate glue and 3D-printer resin. Familiar products can still cause burns, sensitisation, eye injuries, fire or harmful vapour.

## Before using a product

1. Read its label.
2. Obtain the current safety data sheet (SDS).
3. Check hazards, first aid, handling, storage, ventilation, PPE, spill response and disposal.
4. Confirm the activity is permitted in the room.
5. Use the smallest practical quantity.

Keep products in labelled containers. Never decant a chemical into a food or drink container.

## Sensible controls

- Prefer a less hazardous product or process.
- Use local extraction or the designated ventilated area.
- Keep flammable products away from heat, sparks and soldering.
- Wear the exact glove material and eye protection specified; “wear gloves” is not enough because chemicals can pass through unsuitable gloves.
- Keep lids closed and clean spills using the approved procedure.
- Do not mix chemicals unless an assessed procedure specifically requires it.

## Resin printing

Uncured resin and contaminated wash liquid require controlled handling:

- Wear compatible gloves and eye protection.
- Avoid skin contact and splashes.
- Work in the designated ventilated resin area.
- Keep contaminated tools and waste within that workflow.
- Wash and fully cure prints as specified by the manufacturer.
- Never pour resin or contaminated solvent into a sink.

## Adhesives

- Cyanoacrylate bonds skin and eyes rapidly; avoid cotton or wool contact because some formulations can generate heat.
- Hot-melt glue can cause burns and remains hot after leaving the nozzle.
- Spray products need specific ventilation and ignition controls.
- Two-part adhesives require correct proportions and may produce heat while curing.

{: .warning}
> If a chemical is unlabelled, its SDS is unavailable or the spill procedure is unknown, do not use it.

The safety data sheet is the best place to check a product's hazards, first aid, storage and protective equipment.
