# Running a Safe Build Session

This checklist is for workshop leads and club organisers.

## Before people arrive

- Confirm the room booking and permitted activities.
- Review the activity and any room-specific instructions.
- Identify the person responsible for the session.
- Confirm supervision for restricted tools.
- Check exits, assembly point, emergency contact and incident-reporting route.
- Inspect tools, cables, extraction and protective equipment.
- Prepare components with known ratings and undamaged batteries.
- Provide an accessible layout with clear aisles and seated options.
- Plan for safe shutdown and waste collection.

## Opening briefing

Keep it short and specific:

1. Show exits, assembly point and how to summon help.
2. Explain the stop-work signal.
3. Identify hot, sharp, moving, electrical and chemical hazards.
4. Demonstrate isolation, guards, extraction and required PPE.
5. Explain what participants may use independently and what requires supervision.
6. Tell participants to report damage, injury and near misses immediately.

## During the session

- Maintain a reasonable supervisor-to-participant ratio.
- Intervene when a control is missing, not only after an incident.
- Keep beginners away from energised troubleshooting until wiring is checked.
- Watch for fatigue, rushing, crowding and horseplay.
- Pause the whole activity if the same mistake appears repeatedly.
- Provide an alternative task when someone cannot safely use a process.

## Closing checklist

- Turn off and unplug tools as required.
- Return irons to safe storage after cooling.
- Isolate bench supplies and remove batteries from prototypes where practical.
- Account for tools and collect sharp metal waste.
- Separate batteries, electronic waste, resin waste and ordinary rubbish.
- Label safely stored unfinished projects.
- Record defects, incidents and near misses.

## When the plan changes

Re-check the activity if someone introduces a different battery, material, tool, power supply or mechanism. “It is only a small change” is not enough when it changes the available energy or type of hazard.
