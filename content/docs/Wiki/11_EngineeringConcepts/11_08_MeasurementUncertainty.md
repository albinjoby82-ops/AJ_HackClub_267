# Measurement, Accuracy and Uncertainty

A displayed number is not automatically the true value.

- **Accuracy:** closeness to the true value
- **Precision:** repeatability
- **Resolution:** smallest displayed or detectable change
- **Uncertainty:** reasonable range around a result
- **Calibration:** comparison against a known reference

## Good measurement habits

1. Choose the correct instrument and range.
2. Check probe, lead and attenuation settings.
3. Measure the correct points without disturbing the circuit.
4. Repeat the measurement.
5. Record units and conditions.
6. Keep only meaningful digits.

Five readings of `501, 503, 500, 504, 502` have a mean of `502`, with variation of a few counts. Reporting `502.0000` would imply false precision.

## Instruments affect systems

A meter has input resistance. An oscilloscope probe adds resistance and capacitance. A ruler can bend or be misaligned.

Estimate before measuring. A value ten or one hundred times larger than expected often indicates a unit, range, attenuation or decimal-place mistake.
