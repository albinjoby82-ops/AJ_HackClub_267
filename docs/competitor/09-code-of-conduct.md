# Code of conduct

The Dublin Micromouse Open 2026 is a build event for people of every level of
experience, most of whom have never touched a soldering iron. Everyone here is
entitled to a day free of harassment, intimidation and unsafe working.

> made to feel awkward about it.**

## Who this applies to, and where

It applies to competitors, mentors, demonstrators, committee members, judges,
sponsors, guests, photographers and anyone else present, in every space
connected to the Open:

- the venue, including the build area, the maze area, corridors and catering
- online spaces used for the event, including any chat, forum or repository
- social gatherings before, during or after the event day

## What we expect

- Treat people as capable, whatever they know or do not know yet.
- Ask before touching another team's micromouse, laptop, tools or bench.
- Give people room. The maze area and the soldering benches are shared.
- Accept a "no" or a "not now" the first time.
- Use the pronouns and the name a person gives you.
- If someone tells you that you have upset them, stop, listen, and adjust.
- Follow instructions from committee members and demonstrators, especially on
  safety.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, as described below.
- Bullying, belittling, or making someone feel stupid for asking a question.
- Discrimination or slurs relating to gender, gender identity or expression,
  sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, age, disability,
  body size, appearance, or level of experience.
- Sexual attention, comments or imagery in any event space.
- Recording or photographing a person who has asked you not to.
- Sabotage, theft, or interfering with another team's work or equipment.
- Bringing alcohol or other intoxicants into the build or maze areas.
- Ignoring safety instructions, or working in a way that puts others at risk.
- Retaliating against, or pressuring, someone who has raised a concern.

**Harassment, plainly stated:** behaviour towards a person that they do not want
and that a reasonable person would find offensive, humiliating, intimidating or
frightening. It counts whether or not you meant it that way, and it counts if it
happens once. If you are unsure whether something is welcome, it is not yet.

## What happens after a report

1. We listen and write down what you tell us. You will not be asked to confront
   or debate the other person.
2. We take any immediate step needed to make you safe, such as separating
   people, moving a bench, or asking someone to leave the area for a while.
3. Two committee members decide on the response together. Anyone named in the
   report takes no part in that decision.
4. We tell you what we decided. We will keep the report confidential except
   where we must act on it, or where the law requires otherwise.

Depending on what happened, the response may be a quiet word, a formal warning,
removal from an area, disqualification of a competitor or a team, removal from
the venue, or a ban from future UCD ElecSoc events.

**Escalation.** Serious matters are referred on: to UCD under its student
conduct and dignity procedures, and to An Garda Síochána where a criminal
offence may have occurred. We will support anyone who wishes to report to the
Gardaí, and we will never discourage it.

## Safety at a build event

You will be soldering, handling lithium battery packs and using hand tools, in
most cases for the first time. Read the house guidance before the day:

- [Safety and fundamentals](../../content/docs/Wiki/00_MustKnow/index.md)
- [Soldering](../../content/docs/Wiki/01_MakingAndManufacturing/01_01_Soldering.md)
- [Power supplies](../../content/docs/Wiki/02_Equipment/02_01_PowerSupply.md)

On the day: eye protection at the soldering benches, irons back in the stand
every time, no unattended charging, never puncture or short a battery pack, and
stop and fetch a demonstrator if a pack is hot, swollen or damaged. If you are
not sure how to use a tool, ask before you use it. Nobody will think less of
you.
