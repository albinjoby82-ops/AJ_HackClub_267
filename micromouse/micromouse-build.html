S4 · Control and build

Motion and hardware

The algorithm is the easy half. Finishing mice make the robot go where the algorithm said, and know where it is while it does.

01 Motion and turns

Command motion in distance, not time: accelerate at a fixed rate to a cruise speed, hold, then decelerate to arrive at the target. A banked run and a flat-out run should be the same code with a different cruise speed and acceleration.

Build the pivot turn (stop, rotate, go) first as your reliable fallback. Then characterise an arc turn as a fixed, tested manoeuvre with a known entry offset, radius and exit offset, and let the planner call it by name. Do not compute turn geometry during a run.

02 Knowing where you are

Encoder position is wrong: wheels slip and tyre diameters differ. Over a 40-cell run that error puts you in a wall. Use three corrections together.

  • Heading from the IMU. Calibrate the gyro's zero-rate offset with the mouse absolutely still at power-up.
  • Lateral position from the side walls. A PID loop on the left-right difference keeps you centred. It stops working when a wall disappears, so detect that and hold heading instead.
  • Longitudinal position from the front wall. Every visible front wall is a free reset of accumulated distance error.

Sensor discipline. Sample at a fixed rate in a timer. Use hysteresis (two thresholds) for wall present or absent. Read walls at a consistent point in the cell, because a single false wall sends the mouse confidently into a dead end.

03 Hardware decisions for hour one

Everyone gets the same parts. These choices are effectively permanent once you solder.

  • Geometry. Put the wheel axis under the centre of rotation, or every turn also translates the mouse.
  • Mass. Light beats powerful. Where you mount the battery is a handling decision.
  • Width. Narrow is margin for diagonals, not just for fitting.
  • Sensors. Angle side sensors slightly forward, aimed at mid-height on the wall face, so they never see the floor or clip over the top.
  • Power. Battery voltage sags under acceleration. Give the logic a stable rail from the buck converter.
  • Traction. Grip is the real speed limit. If raising acceleration stops helping, this is why.

Component details are in the Hardware guides.
