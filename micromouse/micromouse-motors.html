H6 · Motors and encoders

N20 Motors and Motor A Encoder

The supplied tests drive the left motor through Motor A and the right motor through Motor B. The separate encoder test checks Motor A on GPIO21 and GPIO22.

Use USB to power the controller. Raise the wheels: step 7 and step 8 start automatically. The sketches run at PWM 140 and cap duty at 170/255 for the 9 V supply and 6 V N20 motors. Keep this limit; PWM is not a regulated 6 V supply. Switch off before swapping a motor’s two power wires to correct its direction.

03Motor A encoder wiring
Encoder pin ESP32-C6
C1 GPIO21
C2 GPIO22
VCC 3V3, never the motor battery
GND GND

Use the printed pin labels; the source does not define wire colours or physical connector order. Encoder wires are not required for steps 7 and 8. The supplied encoder sketch tests Motor A only; it does not assign Motor B encoder pins.

04Run the encoder test

Keep the step 7 wiring and upload encoder_test. Raise the wheel before uploading: the motor starts automatically after its startup pause at PWM 85.

Use 115200 baud and No Line Ending. Green means running; red means stopped. The sketch prints a count and ticks/s once per second. Send s to stop, g to go, or z to zero the count.

Result Meaning
Counts increase; ticks/s roughly steady while spinning Encoder is reporting movement
Count settles; ticks/s becomes zero after s Motor stopped
Zero while spinning Check encoder 3V3/GND and C1 connection
Negative ticks/s Power off and swap C1/C2
Count jumps while stopped Check noise from nearby motor wires
05Other motor checks

Use motor_sweep if a motor needs more duty to start. Check a sagging battery or jammed wheel if it hums without turning.

Unplug the motor before pin_test. It drives PWM fully HIGH and would apply the whole 9 V to the 6 V motor.
