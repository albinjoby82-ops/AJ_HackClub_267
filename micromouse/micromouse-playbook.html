S5 · On the day

Playbook and failures

Work in this order and you will always have a mouse that works, only progressively faster.

01 A six-hour playbook
  1. Hour 0 – 1 · Move something

    Blink an LED, then get both motors turning and confirm encoder counts change the right way. Split the team: mechanics, motion, sensors.

  2. Hour 1 – 2 · Drive one cell

    Travel exactly 180 mm and stop, and turn exactly 90° and stop. Repeat each ten times and measure the drift. Everything later depends on these two primitives.

  3. Hour 2 – 3 · See walls

    Print raw sensor values, set thresholds on the real maze under the real lights, add hysteresis, then get the wall-centring loop working.

  4. Hour 3 – 4 · Solve the maze slowly

    Wire flood fill to the movement primitives and search at a deliberately slow speed. Reaching the centre makes you competition-legal.

  5. Hour 4 – 5 · Return, re-run, speed up

    Add the return leg and the speed run. Raise speed in steps until it makes mistakes, then back off one step.

  6. Hour 5 – 6 · Freeze and rehearse

    Stop writing features. Charge the battery and run the full sequence end to end at least twice. Write down which setting is your banked run and which is your gamble.

Above all: a mouse that reliably finishes in 60 seconds beats one that would have done 12 and didn't. Build for a finish first, and always keep a working version to go back to.

02 The ways mice actually fail
  • A phantom wall in the map. Only record a wall from a reading taken at a consistent position in the cell.
  • Accumulated heading error. Correct heading against the walls continuously. Do not trust the gyro alone.
  • A corridor with no side walls. The centring loop steers hard at the gap. Detect it and hold heading.
  • Starting from the wrong place. Make sure a human placing the mouse in a hurry cannot get the start pose wrong.
  • A flat battery mid-slot. Charge between sessions and know how your mouse behaves as voltage falls.
  • An untested return leg. Test turning around inside the goal block.
  • A recovery at the wrong moment. It can wipe the map. Know whether you are willing to pay that before you raise your hand.
